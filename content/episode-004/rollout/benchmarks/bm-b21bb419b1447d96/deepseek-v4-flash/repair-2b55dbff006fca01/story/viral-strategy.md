# Viral Strategy — Devin

## 1. Opening hook (0–3s)

Shot: official demo / overview footage (src-devin-002, src-devin-003). The first frame is an already-started action: the task list advances, the browser tab loads a real GitHub issue, the page scrolls, and the cursor enters the editor. The camera sees change, not a logo.

Synchronous proof: each visible UI change is the proof that the product is acting; the narration names the product type using `claim-devin-001`, `claim-devin-003`, `claim-devin-004`. The visual and the narration do not repeat each other: the picture proves execution, the voice states what kind of system is executing.

## 2. Curiosity gap (by 20s)

At 20 seconds the viewer knows:
- The task was handed off and Devin opened its own browser, scrolled, and started editing (`claim-devin-001/003/004` visible in demo).
- The company was founded by three IOI gold-medal programmers and the product is built to finish tasks for engineers to review (`claim-devin-012`, `claim-devin-009`, `claim-devin-013`).
- The official demo is public.

One open question is carried forward: “What price did the market later put on this AI software engineer?” No second hook question is stacked; the body answers sub-questions only as it moves toward that price.

## 3. Emotional tension

- User desire/choice: the product asks engineers to hand over execution and keep review (`claim-devin-009`). The emotional turn is the choice: how much of the task do you delegate?
- Cost and access: GA team edition was $500/month (`claim-devin-005`); Devin 2.0 made a personal plan start at $20/month and added parallel Devins (`claim-devin-005/006`). This is a real user-facing cost change, not a manufactured crisis.
- Acceptance stakes: Mercedes-Benz used Devin on 200,000+ lines of COBOL and cut an estimated 8-month modernization to 8 days (`claim-devin-007`). The tension is “would a car company trust an AI with legacy code?”, answered by the sourced case.
- No invented emotion, no overpromise.

## 4. Information reveal order

- Reveal 1 (0–3s): product action — visible browser/code execution (`claim-devin-001/003/004`). Evidence: official demo clip. Withheld: who made it and what need it answers.
- Reveal 2 (3–11s): founders and need — three IOI gold-medal programmers, engineers review results (`claim-devin-012/013/009`). Evidence: pull-quote card with Bloomberg attribution; caption with IOI ×3. Withheld: how the first audience saw it.
- Reveal 3 (20–32s): cold start — official demo passed 1.22M views (`claim-devin-015`) and Founders Fund seed round of $21M at $350M valuation came in around launch (`claim-devin-014`). Evidence: YouTube play count overlay + funding stat card with source. Withheld: how access widened.
- Reveal 4 (32–48s): distribution and acceptance — team $500/month, Devin 2.0 personal from $20/month, parallel Devins, Mercedes 8 months → 8 days (`claim-devin-005/006/007`). Evidence: official pricing/blog pages, Devin 2.0 / parallel tutorial video, Mercedes blog and interview. Withheld: the market valuation.
- Reveal 5 (48–60s): market pricing — Cognition raised $1B+ at $25B pre-money / $26B post-money valuation (`claim-devin-016`). Evidence: TechCrunch headline + official financing statement. No new withheld; this answers the hook question.

Strong facts are not introduced without their synchronous evidence. The play count is not converted into adoption; the seed round is not claimed to have caused the valuation.

## 6. Ending payoff

The ending answers the hook question with market pricing, not with a replay of the opening task. Final shot: sourced headline/card showing `2026-05-27`, `$25B pre-money`, `$1B+ raised` (`claim-devin-016`). The narration says the market gave Cognition a $25B pre-money valuation on a raise of more than $1B, attributed to TechCrunch / the company. Stop there; no CTA, no industry trend, no generic “you can do this too” fundraising question.
