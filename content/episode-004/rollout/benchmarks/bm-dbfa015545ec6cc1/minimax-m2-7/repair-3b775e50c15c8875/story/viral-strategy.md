# Devin Viral Strategy

## Opening Hook (0–3 seconds)

**First frame result:** Cursor blinks in Devin's code editor; terminal shows a command completing.

**What the camera sees:** Devin's cloud IDE with editor, terminal, and browser panels visible—not a brand homepage or logo. File auto-saves; terminal prints success output; task progress indicator advances.

**Why 0–3 seconds works:** Anyone who has written code recognizes this moment. No explanation needed that Devin is "an AI software engineer." The visible action comes first; the label comes later.

---

## Curiosity Gap (within 20 seconds)

**By 20 seconds, viewers know:**
- Devin runs code autonomously in its own interface
- Three IOI gold medalists built it to let engineers hand off tasks and review results
- The question viewers are waiting for: "How did the first people discover it, and what did the market eventually pay for this?"

**The single question that drives continued viewing:** Not "can AI replace engineers?" but "what happened when people actually saw it?"

---

## Emotional Tension

**Source:** The tension is not danger—it is delegation. Engineers have always held the keyboard while code runs. Devin removes that requirement, but engineers still own the review. This is a shift in what "doing engineering" means, not a crisis.

**How it is maintained:** Each reveal shows the interface doing something engineers recognize as their own work, then reveals the human choice that built it, then the scale of adoption, then the market price. The emotional note stays in curiosity and re-evaluation, not alarm.

---

## Information Reveal Order

### Reveal 1 (0:00–0:03): Visible Autonomous Execution
**What is shown:** Code auto-saves; terminal command completes; Devin's own interface panels active.
**Withheld:** Who made it, what problem they solved.
**Purpose:** Establish the core capability with zero background required.
**Visual:** Devin's cloud IDE—editor, terminal, browser in one frame.
**Source:** claim-devin-001, claim-devin-004; src-devin-002, src-devin-003.

### Reveal 2 (0:03–0:20): Founders and Their Problem
**What is shown:** Three IOI gold medalists; product is positioned as handing tasks to Devin, engineers review results.
**Withheld:** How did the first people see it?
**Purpose:** Answers "who built it" and "what problem they were solving" with verifiable credentials and official positioning.
**Visual:** Founders in interview (src-devin-017); Devin's task-planning sidebar showing the hand-off workflow.
**Source:** claim-devin-012, claim-devin-009, claim-devin-013.

### Reveal 3 (0:20–0:32): Cold Start—Demo and Seed Funding
**What is shown:** Official demo has 1.22M+ views; Founders Fund invested $21M seed at $350M valuation.
**Withheld:** How did price and access change? Did enterprises accept it?
**Purpose:** Answers "how did first people see it" with independently verifiable view count and early funding.
**Visual:** YouTube view counter (src-devin-002); Founders Fund citation from TechCrunch.
**Source:** claim-devin-015, claim-devin-014.

### Reveal 4 (0:32–0:48): Pricing Tiers and Enterprise Acceptance
**What is shown:** December 2024 team tier $500/month; April 2025 Devin 2.0 personal from $20/month plus parallel Devins; Mercedes-Benz gave Devin 200,000 lines of COBOL, finished in 8 days vs. 8 months estimated.
**Withheld:** What is the market price of this capability?
**Purpose:** Shows pricing evolution and enterprise validation through a specific, sourced case study—not speculation.
**Visual:** Mercedes dev team interview (src-devin-011); Devin 2.0 pricing page; parallel Devin UI (src-devin-014).
**Source:** claim-devin-005, claim-devin-006, claim-devin-007.

### Reveal 5 (0:48–1:00): Capital Market Verdict
**What is shown:** May 2026—Cognition raised over $1B at $25B pre-money valuation.
**Withheld:** None. Market has spoken.
**Purpose:** Closes with a class of evidence (capital markets) that could not have been anticipated from the opening terminal.
**Visual:** TechCrunch financing report, official Cognition blog (src-devin-007, src-devin-008).
**Source:** claim-devin-016.

## Ending Payoff

**How the ending answers the opening:** The opening shows "it runs code on its own." The ending answers "the market valued that capability at $25 billion." The payoff is a new, larger class of evidence—capital market pricing—not a repetition of the terminal action.

**Why this does not loop back to the opening:** The terminal result and the valuation belong to different logical categories. The first is a product demonstration; the second is the market's independent judgment on what that product category is worth. Audiences arrive with the question "can it do that?" and leave knowing "the market said yes, at this price."

**Capital market evidence used:**
- $25 billion pre-money valuation (TechCrunch, company-reported May 2026)
- Over $1 billion raised (TechCrunch, company-reported May 2026)

**No interactive CTA.** No trend escalation.** No generic riches question ("you could profit too").** No market prediction.
