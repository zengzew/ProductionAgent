## Opening Hook (0–3 s)

**First frame:** Devin's cloud IDE fills the screen — browser pane at top, code editor center, terminal bottom. The cursor inside the browser pane moves. A URL field fills character by character. A page begins to load inside Devin's own interface.

**0–3 s action:** Cursor move → URL fill → page load initiation — all inside Devin's UI, no human hand visible, no keyboard input shown.

**Same-3s proof:** Single static frame containing all three panes (browser, code editor, terminal). Source: Devin's official interface shown in the official demo (src-devin-002, src-devin-003). The camera sees a result — a page loading inside an AI's own environment — not a product logo or a static screenshot labeled "it works."

---

## Curiosity Gap (within 20 s)

**Viewer already knows at 20 s:**
1. Devin opens its own browser and loads a page — a visible, zero-background result.
2. Three IOI gold medalists built it so that an AI takes a task and gets it done autonomously.
3. The product is positioned as a teammate that completes work for an engineer to review.

**Viewer is still waiting to answer:** What did the market pay for this capability?

**How the question is kept alive:** The narration at 11–20 s names the product capability (browser-open, task-complete) and explicitly defers to the market answer: "That capability found a price. What was it?" No other open questions remain in the frame at 20 s.

---

## Emotional Tension

**Source of tension:** Not a crisis or failure — a choice. Engineers have always sat at their own terminals writing code themselves. The three founders chose to build an executioner that works autonomously. The tension lives in the question every engineer faces: "Do I keep doing it myself, or do I hand it off?"

**This tension is sustained by:** Each reveal advances the scope of the choice — from visible action (beat 1) to founder intent (beat 2) to market adoption signals (beats 3–4). The tension resolves not with a dramatic reversal but with a market number: $2.5B valuation, $1B+ raise. That number answers "the market chose to pay this much for the choice to hand off work."

**What is NOT used:** No manufactured urgency, no founder testimonials about vision, no implication that engineers who don't use it are falling behind.

## Ending Payoff

**What the ending answers:** "What price did the market put on this?" — the question left open at 11–20 seconds.

**Sourced evidence used:** $2.5B pre-money valuation (26B post-money), raised over $1B, May 27 2026. Source: TechCrunch (src-devin-007) reporting on company-announced figures; confirmed by company blog (src-devin-008). This is capital market evidence — a price set by investors — not a claim about user count, retention, or business revenue.

**What does NOT appear in the ending:**
- Return to the opening browser action (beat 1 is not revisited; the valuation is new information)
- Any CTA ("try it now," "join the waitlist")
- Industry trend升华 ("AI coding is the future")
- Wealth prompt ("you could profit from this")
- Forward projection of the valuation trajectory

**Ending frame:** Capital market number. Fade or cut to black. No narration extends beyond the sourced number.
