# Three-Act Structure — Episode 004: Devin

**Target runtime:** 55–70 seconds (60s ± 5s with TTS pauses and end-frame hold)
**Core question:** How do we know Devin actually works as an AI software engineer?

---

## Act 1: The Execution (0–20 seconds)

**Narrative task:** Define the product through visible action. Give the viewer something they can verify.

**Visible action:**
- Devin's interface appears: task input field, browser panel, code editor, terminal.
- A task is entered. Devin begins working.
- The browser panel shows a page loading. The editor shows code appearing. The terminal shows a command running.
- Each step is timestamped or labeled. Nothing is off-screen.

**Evidence source:** Official demo video (src-devin-002) — Devin executing a task with visible steps. Also: Getting Started tutorial (src-devin-003) for the interface layout.

**Claim IDs:** claim-devin-001 (autonomous execution with browser, editor, terminal), claim-devin-003 (interface components), claim-devin-004 (visible steps)

**Story move:** The viewer now has a concrete mental model. Devin is not autocomplete. Devin is not a chatbot. Devin is an autonomous executor with its own tools.

**Rhythm:** 0–3s: Interface appears. 3–10s: Task entered, execution begins. 10–20s: Steps are shown, the question is implied—"can it really do this?"

---

## Act 2: The Evidence (20–45 seconds)

**Narrative task:** Answer the viewer's implicit doubt: "Is this just a demo, or does it work in the real world?"

**Two independent evidence layers:**

### Layer 1 — Enterprise Trust (20–30s)
- **Fact:** Mercedes-Benz used Devin on a COBOL modernization project. Over 200,000 lines of code. Estimated 8 months → completed in 8 days.
- **Source:** Official Mercedes partnership blog (src-devin-006) + official customer video (src-devin-011)
- **Claim IDs:** claim-devin-007
- **Visuals:** Code volume figure (200K lines), timeline result (8 months → 8 days). Show the real project scope, not a generic "enterprise customer."
- **Rhythm:** 20–25s: Project scope introduced. 25–30s: Result shown with specific numbers.

### Layer 2 — Insider Adoption (30–40s)
- **Fact:** Cognition's own engineers now have Devin commit 89% of the company's code.
- **Source:** Official Series D blog "More Devins in More Places" (src-devin-008)
- **Claim IDs:** claim-devin-008
- **Visuals:** "89% of code committed by Devin" label or direct quote from the blog.
- **Rhythm:** 30–35s: Introduce insider context. 35–40s: Show the 89% figure.

**Story move:** Two independent evidence types answer two different doubts. "Does it work for big enterprise projects?" (Mercedes, 200K lines) + "Do the people who built it actually trust it?" (89% internal code). Together they cover external and internal validation.

**What is NOT in Act 2:**
- No version history. Devin 1.0 → Devin 2.0 chronology is not the story.
- No PR merge rate improvement or 4× speed (claim-devin-011; not in narration).
- No SWE-bench numbers (claim-devin-010; not in narration).

---

## Act 3: The Market Verdict (45–65 seconds)

**Narrative task:** Close with a concrete, source-matched capital markets outcome. Let the market answer the capability question.

**Visible action:**
- Funding figure appears: "Over $1 billion raised at $2.5 billion pre-money valuation."
- Source label: TechCrunch reporting (src-devin-007) + Cognition official Series D blog (src-devin-008)
- Claim IDs: claim-devin-016

**Rhythm:** 45–55s: Funding announcement with valuation. 55–65s: Hold on the figure. TTS pause.

**Story move:** Markets price products. The viewer has seen the execution (Act 1) and the evidence (Act 2). The $2.5B pre-money valuation is the external, capital-verified answer to "how do we know it works?"—not a claim by Cognition, not a benchmark, but what investors collectively decided the product is worth.

**What is NOT in Act 3:**
- No forward-looking statements ("Devin is improving every day").
- No industry trend language ("AI is transforming software").
- No comparison to competitors.

---

## Information Additions Per Act (Relative to Previous Act)

| Transition | New Information Added | Relation |
|---|---|---|
| Act 1 → Act 2 | Mercedes case with specific COBOL volume and timeline | New evidence type: external enterprise trust |
| Act 2 → Act 3 | Internal adoption metric (89%) already added; Act 3 adds market pricing | New evidence type: capital markets valuation |

Each act adds a distinct, non-redundant evidence type. No act merely restates the previous.

---

## Narrative Chain (Causal vs. Sequence)

1. **Visible execution** → establishes product capability
   - **Claim IDs:** claim-devin-001, claim-devin-004
   - **Relation:** causal (seeing the product execute directly demonstrates capability)

2. **Mercedes enterprise case** → external validation
   - **Claim IDs:** claim-devin-007
   - **Relation:** sequence-only (enterprise adoption followed product launch; not proven as direct result of demo views)

3. **89% internal code** → insider adoption
   - **Claim IDs:** claim-devin-008
   - **Relation:** sequence-only (internal adoption co-occurs with product maturity; independent of Mercedes case)

4. **$2.5B valuation** → market pricing
   - **Claim IDs:** claim-devin-016
   - **Relation:** sequence-only (valuation reflects accumulated evidence; not causal on any single claim)

## End Frame

The final shot holds on the $2.5B pre-money valuation figure. This is a source-matched fact from TechCrunch and Cognition's own Series D announcement. It does not predict the future. It answers the core question with what markets decided, based on the evidence the viewer has already seen.

No text overlay beyond the valuation figure. No voiceover restart of the product definition. No "and Devin is just getting started."
