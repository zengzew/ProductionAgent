# Story Angle — Episode 004: Devin

## Selected Angle: Product-in-Action

**This product promises** autonomous task execution that delivers finished, reviewable software engineering work.

**The evidence shows that promise through** visible step-by-step execution in Devin's own interface, then validates it with a named enterprise customer case (Mercedes-Benz: 200,000 lines of COBOL, estimated 8 months → 8 days) and insider adoption (89% of Cognition's own code committed by Devin), closing on the market pricing that followed ($2.5B pre-money valuation).

---

## Why This Angle

The zero-background viewer does not know what Devin is. The default mental model for "AI coding tool" is either autocomplete (VS Code Copilot) or a chatbot that suggests code. Both are wrong for Devin.

Devin's product promise is that it independently executes a software engineering task and delivers output an engineer can review. This is a fundamentally different product category. It cannot be explained in abstractions. It must be shown.

The chosen angle makes the product definition the first thing the viewer sees: Devin's own interface, executing its own steps. Only after the viewer understands what Devin does do we provide evidence that the output is trusted at scale.

---

## Alternative Angles Considered

### Angle B — Competitive Programmer Advantage

**This product promises** that elite competitive programmers have a structural advantage in building AI that reasons through complex software engineering decisions.

**Evidence shows this through** three IOI gold medalists founding Cognition, a viral demo that generated 122万+ views, enterprise adoption, and a $2.5B valuation.

**Why rejected:** The origin story explains why Cognition was well-positioned to build Devin, but it does not prove Devin works. A viewer who asks "does this actually work?" does not get an answer from "the founders were Olympiad champions." The product evidence must come first.

### Angle C — Pricing Democratization

**This product promises** enterprise-grade autonomous coding at a personal price point.

**Evidence shows this through** the $500/month team tier at GA, the $20/month personal tier in Devin 2.0, and the scaling adoption that followed.

**Why rejected:** Pricing is a distribution mechanism, not a capability proof. The viewer who sees $20/month but has no evidence that Devin actually produces working code has learned nothing about product quality. Pricing cannot substitute for the "does it work?" answer.

## What the Viewer Learns and When

| Time | Information | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10s | Devin accepts a task and executes it visibly: browser, editor, terminal | Defines the product. No abstraction. |
| 10–20s | The execution is real, step-by-step, reviewable | Addresses the skeptic's doubt: "Is this just a demo?" |
| 20–30s | Mercedes trusted Devin on 200K lines of COBOL; estimated 8 months → 8 days | First evidence of enterprise-scale trust |
| 30–40s | Cognition's own engineers have Devin commit 89% of their code | Second evidence: insider trust, not just marketing |
| 40–50s | Devin can run parallel instances, each with its own IDE | Capability expansion; not the core proof but contextualizes scale |
| 50–60s | May 2026: $1B+ raised at $2.5B pre-money valuation | Market verdict. The product is priced by investors, not just described. |

---

## Misconception to Correct

**Natural misconception:** "AI coding tool" = autocomplete or chat-with-code-suggestion.

**Correction:** Devin is an autonomous agent. It opens its own browser, writes its own code, runs its own tests, and produces a finished pull request for an engineer to review. It does not suggest; it executes.

---

## Tension Without Villain

The tension in this story is not "Devin vs. a threat." It is "the viewer doubts whether AI can actually do software engineering work, and we answer with visible proof."

The product itself creates the dramatic question: "Can it really do that?" The evidence answers it.

---

## What Must Not Appear in This Angle

- Any claim that Devin works without human oversight
- SWE-bench scores as proof of real-world success
- The Mercedes result described as a measured before/after (it is an estimate vs. outcome)
- Version-by-version chronology of Devin releases
- Industry-wide adoption statistics without company-specific attribution
