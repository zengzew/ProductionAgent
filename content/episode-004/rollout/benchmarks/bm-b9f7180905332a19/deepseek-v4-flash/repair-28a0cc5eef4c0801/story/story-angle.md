# Story Angle — episode-004: Devin

## Candidate angle 1 — The founders' algorithm
Use the three IOI gold medalists and Scott Wu's quote as the center. It could explain why this team could attack software engineering as a decision problem (claim-devin-012/013). It fails as the spine because we have no sourced founder motivation or struggle; it risks turning a capability into a personality story.

## Candidate angle 2 — Watch it work (chosen)
Use the launch demo as the first proof: Devin accepts a task, opens a browser, edits files, runs commands, and shows every step (claim-devin-001/003/004). This angle lets a zero-background viewer see the product before hearing history. The rest of the story answers who built it, how it was seen, how access widened, and what market proof followed.

## Candidate angle 3 — The price collapse
Use $500/month team pricing to $20/month individual plan as the hook. It is attention-grabbing but risks the exact misconception the sources correct: the two prices were different plans, not the same plan's drop (claim-devin-005, src-devin-005/009). It also puts pricing before product understanding, which hurts the 20-second product model.

## Chosen angle
- Product promise: Devin promises to turn a software task into visible, independent work that a human reviews.
- Evidence shows that promise through the official demo, 1.22M+ public views, Devin 2.0's wider access, Mercedes-Benz's 8-months-to-8-days case, Cognition's 89% self-use figure, and a $2.5B pre-money financing.
- Tension, not crisis: the real tension is between the old job of writing code step by step and the new job of handing a whole task to a visible agent and reviewing the result.

## Why not another angle
- We do not have sourced evidence for founder motivation, user adoption causes, or a low point; no fabricated crisis can carry the story.
- The product's visible autonomy is the strongest independently verified material, so it leads.
